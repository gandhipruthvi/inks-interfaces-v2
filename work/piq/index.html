// the challenge

Caffeine, lemonade, and an exclamation mark.

What needed to change

Energy drinks look like energy drinks: The category is a wall of black cans and neon angles. Hard to stand out by joining the chorus. | Lemonade looks like a kids&#39; drink: Pastel pink, hand-drawn lemons, summer-camp lettering. Not the brief either. | Three formats, one face: A can, a glass bottle, and a squeeze pouch — each one wants to look like a different brand by default. | Caffeine credibility: The brand has to whisper &#39;this works&#39; without writing it on the can in screaming yellow.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

Lemon-first colour: The brand&#39;s green is the colour of an under-ripe lemon — not the cliché yellow. | Confident type, no shouting: A single condensed sans does all the typography. No display flourishes, no italics. | Photography over illustration: Every brand asset is a real product, real ice, real water. The drink earns its photo. | Format-modular: Can, bottle, squeeze — same wordmark, same proportion, three completely different containers.

// strategic thesis

Lemon. Caffeine. An exclamation mark — earned, not added.

Cohesive omnichannel system

From screen taps to shipping box unboxing, the identity remains unmistakably unified.
