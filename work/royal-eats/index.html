// the challenge

A modern identity for a beloved snack brand.

What needed to change

The food outran the brand: Five-star food, three-star packaging. Repeat customers loved it; new ones didn&#39;t know what they were looking at. | Delivery, but dressed for dining: The packaging had to survive a forty-minute ride and still feel like a gift on arrival. | Heritage without kitsch: Hyderabadi cuisine has a 500-year history. Most brands lean on Mughal silhouettes and gold. Royal Eats needed heritage without the cliché. | One identity, two rooms: The brand had to read at sixty miles an hour on a delivery bike and at six feet on a flagship facade.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

Heritage, not pageantry: Reference the Nizam era without copying its ornament. The brand should feel old without looking dated. | Built for travel: Every piece of packaging is designed to look better after a forty-minute delivery ride, not worse. | One face, two rooms: The same wordmark works on a delivery sticker at three centimetres and on a façade at three metres. | Quiet luxury: No gold foil. No script flourishes. A single deep red doing all the heavy lifting.

// strategic thesis

A 500-year-old cuisine, served in a brand that finally fits.
