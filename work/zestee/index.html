// the challenge

From juice bar to destination café.

What needed to change

Three locations, three brands: Each café had been opened in a hurry. Every signboard, takeaway cup, and Instagram grid told a slightly different story. | Mistaken for a juice bar: Drop-in customers ordered juice and left. The coffee program — the actual business — was invisible from the street. | Forgotten on Instagram: No one geo-tagged Zestee. The interiors were beautiful but the brand never asked to be photographed. | No system, no scale: Opening café four would mean rebuilding café one from scratch. There was no kit of parts to copy.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

Warmth, not luxury: Terracotta and cream beat black and gold. Zestee should feel like a friend&#39;s living room, not a hotel lobby. | Hand over machine: Hand-painted signage. Hand-stamped sleeves. The brand wears its making process. | Type-led: A single custom display face does the heavy lifting. No stock fonts, no decorative flourish. | Photographable: Every cup, plate, and sticker is designed first as a flat-lay. The brand earns its Instagram.

// strategic thesis

Built to be photographed. Designed to be returned to.

// craft principles

three takeaways from this build.

01

Front-of-pack clarity

State the honest truth before the branding. Customers buy what they understand.

02

Tactile materials build trust

Subtle textures, weight, and finishes tell the customer you care about detail everywhere.

03

Cohesive omnichannel system

From screen taps to shipping box unboxing, the identity remains unmistakably unified.
