// the challenge

A media brand built to be readable by everyone.

What needed to change

  • Accessibility is usually a retrofitMost brands and sites add accessibility at QA — colour swaps, alt-text scans, late aria-labels. That order produces worse results than designing for it from the start.
  • Brand and website, two timelinesPaul wanted identity and site to ship together. The visual system had to be settled before any page was laid out.
  • AA-clean without becoming blandAccessibility constraints often produce visually flat brands. Lab Coat needed presence — high contrast had to feel intentional, not regulatory.
  • Founder-led, but enterprise-crediblePaul is the practice. The brand had to feel like a serious media operation while staying close to a single founder voice.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

  • Contrast as compositionEvery colour pair clears AA. Constraint becomes the system's rhythm — high-contrast type, generous spacing, no decorative subtlety.
  • Keyboard first, mouse secondEvery page navigable by Tab. Focus states are designed objects, not browser defaults.
  • Voice on the pageCopy reads like Paul talks. Plain sentences, short paragraphs, no agency jargon. Reads aloud well on a screen reader.
  • One green, one black, one paperThree colours, on purpose. Accessibility becomes the design system, not its constraint.

// strategic thesis

Lab Coat Media is Paul's media practice. We designed the brand identity and the website together — built from the start around WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility and inclusivity, ensuring a seamless experience for all users rather than retrofitting it later.
