// the challenge

A neighbourhood bakery, treated like an heirloom.

What needed to change

Twelve years of sticker: The brand was a hand-cut sticker applied to brown bread bags. No system, no consistency, no scale. | Two bakeries, two postcodes: The original Alfama bakery and the new Príncipe Real one couldn&#39;t look like clones — but had to feel like the same family. | Bread looks bad in photos: Most bakery brands lean on stock photos of impossible loaves. Inês refused. The brand had to make her actual bread the hero. | The sticker WAS the loyalty: Customers had grown attached to the imperfect handmade sticker. Replacing it with a designed system risked losing what people loved.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

Honest crust: Every photo is real bread baked that morning. No retouching, no impossible browns. | Keep the sticker spirit: The new wordmark has a hand-stamped quality. It&#39;s a system that still looks like one person made it. | Bag is the billboard: The bread bag is the most-seen surface. The whole system is tuned for the walk home. | Two sites, one rhythm: Each bakery has a slightly different signage scale. Same wordmark, different size — like siblings, not clones.

// strategic thesis

The loaf was the brand for twelve years. Now they finally match.
