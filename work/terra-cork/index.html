// the challenge

Cork, finally treated like a material instead of a fix.

What needed to change

  • Cork looks cheap by defaultDecades of bulletin boards and shoe insoles. The brand had to reframe cork as a premium material, not a fallback.
  • Sixteen product formatsCoasters, planters, trays, soap dishes, wallets, table mats, candle holders, organisers. One identity has to hold across all of them.
  • Sustainable without preachingCork is genuinely one of the most sustainable materials on earth. The brand had to communicate that without becoming an eco-lecture.
  • Founder-led, but not founder-frontedAkshit didn't want his face on the brand. The material had to be the hero. Personality had to come from the product, not the founder.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

  • Material as the heroEvery photograph leads with the cork itself — texture, grain, weight. The product is the supporting actor.
  • Restrained typeA single warm serif does all the typography. Cork has enough texture already.
  • Earth palette onlyNo greens, no blues. The whole brand sits in ochre, sand, terracotta, and oak — the tones cork already lives in.
  • Sustainability stated, not shoutedProvenance and harvest cycle printed on every label. Once. Never repeated in marketing copy.

// strategic thesis

Slow material. Patient design. A thing that's still here in fifty years.
