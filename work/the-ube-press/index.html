// the challenge

A modern café rooted in heritage.

What needed to change

  • Trend vs. heritageMost ube products lean into the Instagram colour. The Reyeses wanted heritage credibility, not a viral moment.
  • The flavour syrup problemNinety percent of Western ube products are syrup-flavoured. The Ube Press uses the real root. The brand has to make that obvious.
  • Two markets, one faceFilipino diaspora customers and curious-Western customers want different stories. The brand has to be both.
  • Purple is loudUbe's natural colour is non-negotiable. The brand has to wear it without becoming cartoonish.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

  • Single colour, single heroThe whole brand is built around one ube purple. Every other element is restrained to let it lead.
  • Tagalog as first languageThe wordmark includes a Tagalog tagline. The diaspora reads it first; everyone else reads it next.
  • Hand over machineHand-drawn label illustrations, hand-stamped batch numbers. The brand wears its labour.
  • Root, not flavourEvery label calls out the farm. The product's truth is the brand's strategy.

// strategic thesis

Real root. Real hands. A real grandmother somewhere in Bohol.
