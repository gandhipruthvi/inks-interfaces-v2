// the challenge

A nostalgic Indian icon, rebuilt for its next decade.

What needed to change

The brand is younger than the buyer: The current illustration style reads as childlike. The actual buyer is a thirty-five-year-old paying premium for nostalgia they trust. | Range is fifteen tiny stories: Every SKU has its own folktale on the back. Beautiful — but the brand has lost a single voice in favour of fifteen voices. | International shelves don&#39;t speak Hindi: Pack copy designed for a Bombay grocery doesn&#39;t read in Toronto. The brand needs a single hook that survives translation. | Nostalgia has an expiry date: The generation Paper Boat was designed for is now in their forties. The next decade of growth comes from a generation that wasn&#39;t promised these flavours.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

Keep the boat, lose the diorama: The paper-boat mark stays. The crowded illustrated backgrounds go. The wordmark earns the pack on its own. | One story per range, not one per SKU: Regional clusters (summers, monsoon, festive) carry the narrative. Individual SKUs get clean, confident labels. | Heritage tones, modern weights: Existing palette stays in the family. Type weights and grid get the upgrade. | Translatable hook on every pack: A single English-Hindi cultural anchor on every pack — survives any market without becoming exoticised.

// strategic thesis

The same drinks. The same memories. A brand grown up by half a decade.

// craft principles

three takeaways from this build.

01

Front-of-pack clarity

State the honest truth before the branding. Customers buy what they understand.

02

Tactile materials build trust

Subtle textures, weight, and finishes tell the customer you care about detail everywhere.

03

Cohesive omnichannel system

From screen taps to shipping box unboxing, the identity remains unmistakably unified.
