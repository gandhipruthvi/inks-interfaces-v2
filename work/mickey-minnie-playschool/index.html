// the challenge

A playful rebrand for a beloved playschool.

What needed to change

Designed for admin, used by parent: Every flow assumed an admin user. Parents had to navigate seventeen screens to find a single piece of information. | WhatsApp was winning: Parents stayed on WhatsApp because it was faster. The app had to feel as fast as a message, not as slow as a portal. | Pickup-line UX: The app&#39;s heaviest use happens one-handed, in a car, at 2:50pm. The product had to be designed for that exact moment. | Four campuses, one app: Each campus had different schedules, fee structures, and teachers. The product couldn&#39;t fragment by location.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

One screen, three answers: The parent home shows today&#39;s update, today&#39;s pickup time, and today&#39;s fee status. Three answers, no taps. | Faster than WhatsApp: Every common action — pay, view, message teacher — takes under fifteen seconds. The app has to outrun the chat group. | Big targets, low-light: All tap targets sized for one-handed use in low light. The app is opened in cars, not at desks. | Joyful, not childish: Bright primary colour palette. Playful illustration. The brand is for the kid; the UX is for the adult.

// strategic thesis

Design for the moments that matter, from the classroom to the playground.

three takeaways from this build.

01

Front-of-pack clarity

State the honest truth before the branding. Customers buy what they understand.

02

Tactile materials build trust

Subtle textures, weight, and finishes tell the customer you care about detail everywhere.

03

Cohesive omnichannel system

From screen taps to shipping box unboxing, the identity remains unmistakably unified.
