// the challenge

A childhood favourite, rebuilt for the gym.

What needed to change

  • Equity, not erasureStrip too much of the original Kit-Kat and you lose the icon. Strip too little and you look like a healthwash.
  • Macros are the new ingredientsBuyers read the back of the pack before the front. The macro callout has to do the work the flavour name used to.
  • Gym shelf, not snack aisleThe wrapper has to survive next to MyProtein, RXBAR, and Barebells. Different vocabulary, different rhythm.
  • Stay snackableFunctional bars are tasted, not just consumed. The brand can't forget that the original Kit-Kat is delicious.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

  • Keep the redThe Kit-Kat red is forty percent of the brand. Lose it and you've designed a different product.
  • Front-of-pack honestyMacros live where the flavour used to. No fake leaves, no athletic silhouettes.
  • Snap-first photographyEvery shot leads with the broken bar. The product earned its photo.
  • Modular for SKUsThe wrapper system flexes across six flavours and three protein levels without redrawing the logo.

// strategic thesis

Same break. Different reason to take it.
