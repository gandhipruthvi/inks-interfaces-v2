// the challenge

A pet brand designed for the human who actually buys it.

What needed to change

  • Pet branding is for dogsMost brands lean on cartoon paws and pet-cliché photography. The actual buyer — a human reading ingredient panels — gets nothing.
  • Ingredient claims vs. shelf appealHero ingredients on the front of the pack matter — but most brands bury them in tiny back-panel print.
  • Food, treats, accessoriesA kibble bag and a bandana have nothing in common physically. The brand has to feel like one company across both.
  • Modern, not corporateMass pet brands feel sterile. DTC pet brands feel hip. Waggle needed warmth without becoming a startup cliché.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

  • Front-of-pack honestyProtein and source named in 48pt type on the front. Buyers see the ingredient before the brand.
  • Illustrated, not photographedMascot illustration replaces stock dog photography. Owns the brand without faking another dog's photo.
  • Warm systemCream base, single orange accent. Every SKU sits in the same warm palette. Shelf reads as a family.
  • Modular across formatsSame wordmark on a kibble bag, a bandana, and a toy. Three completely different products, one face.

// strategic thesis

Real ingredients. Real bag. A dog who'd recognise the food from across the room.
