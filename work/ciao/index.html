// the challenge

Wood-fired Naples, on a side street in Bopal.

What needed to change

  • Italian food in India is a costumeMost Italian restaurants here use red-checkered tablecloths, Toscana fonts, and over-decorated menus. Ciao needed to feel Italian without the theatre.
  • Limited stock is the model, not the apologyCiao runs out of dough by 8:30pm every night — and that's the point. The brand had to communicate scarcity as care, not failure.
  • Open kitchen as the brandWatching the dough get stretched is the experience. The brand had to make the kitchen the hero — not push it to a corner.
  • Authentic without the lectureNeapolitan credentials matter. But customers don't want a history lesson. The brand had to wear authenticity quietly.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

  • Type-led, not illustratedThe whole brand is a single Italian serif and a generous grid. No fake Tuscan villas, no flag-coloured ribbons.
  • Olive green over redThe palette deliberately avoids Italian-flag red. Reads grown-up, not themed.
  • Menu as the brand surfaceThe single-page menu is the most-touched piece of collateral. Treated like a printed object — not a laminated leaflet.
  • Cap as design featureEvery printed piece names the daily dough cap. Scarcity stated, never apologised for.

// strategic thesis

Ciao Pizzeria is an authentic Neapolitan wood-fired pizza joint in Ahmedabad. The pies sell out by 8:30pm every night. We built the brand so the queue makes sense.
