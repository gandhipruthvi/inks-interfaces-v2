// the challenge

Wood-fired Naples, on a side street in Bopal.

What needed to change

Italian food in India is a costume: Most Italian restaurants here use red-checkered tablecloths, Toscana fonts, and over-decorated menus. Ciao needed to feel Italian without the theatre. | Limited stock is the model, not the apology: Ciao runs out of dough by 8:30pm every night — and that&#39;s the point. The brand had to communicate scarcity as care, not failure. | Open kitchen as the brand: Watching the dough get stretched is the experience. The brand had to make the kitchen the hero — not push it to a corner. | Authentic without the lecture: Neapolitan credentials matter. But customers don&#39;t want a history lesson. The brand had to wear authenticity quietly.

// strategic positioning

Designing for the human decision maker, not an algorithm.

Type-led, not illustrated: The whole brand is a single Italian serif and a generous grid. No fake Tuscan villas, no flag-coloured ribbons. | Olive green over red: The palette deliberately avoids Italian-flag red. Reads grown-up, not themed. | Menu as the brand surface: The single-page menu is the most-touched piece of collateral. Treated like a printed object — not a laminated leaflet. | Cap as design feature: Every printed piece names the daily dough cap. Scarcity stated, never apologised for.

// strategic thesis

Ciao Pizzeria is an authentic Neapolitan wood-fired pizza joint in Ahmedabad. The pies sell out by 8:30pm every night. We built the brand so the queue makes sense.

three takeaways from this build.

Front-of-pack clarity

State the honest truth before the branding. Customers buy what they understand.

02

03

Cohesive omnichannel system

From screen taps to shipping box unboxing, the identity remains unmistakably unified.
